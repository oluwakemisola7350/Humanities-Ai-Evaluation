# Sources

This document records the sources used to develop and evaluate the questions in this project. Sources were selected to provide official statistics, institutional economic analysis, and independent reporting on Nigeria's fuel-price reforms, inflation, and household economic conditions.

## Source 1: International Monetary Fund

**Title:** 2026 Article IV Consultation with Nigeria  
**Organization:** International Monetary Fund (IMF)  
**Publication date:** June 9, 2026  
**Type:** Institutional economic analysis  
**URL:** [https://www.imf.org/en/publications/cr/issues/2026/06/08/nigeria-2026-article-iv-consultation-press-release-staff-report-and-statement-by-the-576681]

### Why this source was selected

The IMF report provides analysis of Nigeria's recent economic reforms, inflation, macroeconomic conditions, and the effects of changes in global fuel, food, and fertilizer prices.

### Evidence used in this project

- The report discusses improvements in Nigeria's macroeconomic stability following recent reforms.
- It explains that higher global fuel, food, and fertilizer prices can create inflationary pressures.
- It also discusses how higher commodity prices can affect exports and government revenues through different economic channels.
- The report discusses risks to poverty and food security.

### Evaluation use

This source is primarily used to test:

- causal reasoning
- understanding of economic mechanisms
- evidence-based interpretation
- ability to distinguish multiple effects from a single economic event


## Source 2: World Bank

**Title:** Nigeria Development Update, April 2026: Nigeria's Tomorrow Must Start Today: The Case for Early Childhood Development  
**Organization:** World Bank  
**Publication date:** April 7, 2026  
**Type:** Development/economic analysis  
**URL:** [https://openknowledge.worldbank.org/entities/publication/87d25440-6887-44dc-a83c-fdb931a9b7ee]

### Why this source was selected

The World Bank report provides evidence on Nigeria's recent macroeconomic stabilization and examines whether improvements in macroeconomic conditions have translated into improved household welfare.

### Evidence used in this project

- The report discusses Nigeria's economic growth and recent stabilization.
- It reports that inflation had declined substantially by February 2026.
- It notes that household incomes had not fully recovered.
- It notes that poverty remained high.
- It discusses the relationship between macroeconomic stability, employment, household welfare, and living standards.

### Evaluation use

This source is primarily used to test:

- contextual reasoning
- interpretation of economic indicators
- distinction between macroeconomic performance and household welfare
- ability to avoid unsupported conclusions



## Source 3: Reuters

**Title:** In deluge of protests, fuel subsidies prove hard to abolish  
**Organization:** Reuters  
**Publication date:** August 8, 2024  
**Type:** News report  
**URL:** [https://www.reuters.com/world/africa/deluge-protests-fuel-subsidies-prove-hard-abolish-2024-08-08/]

### Why this source was selected

The Reuters report provides contemporary reporting on Nigeria's fuel-subsidy removal, petrol prices, transportation costs, inflationary pressures, and reactions to the reforms.

### Evidence used in this project

- The report describes the removal of the fuel subsidy and subsequent changes in petrol prices.
- It discusses the effects of higher fuel costs on transportation and household expenses.
- It presents perspectives from people and experts regarding the economic effects of the reforms.

### Evaluation use

This source is primarily used to test:

- source interpretation
- causal reasoning
- distinction between reported observations and established causal claims
- use of contextual evidence



## Source 4: National Bureau of Statistics

**Title:** Consumer Price Index (CPI) — February 2026  
**Organization:** National Bureau of Statistics (NBS)  
**Publication date:** February 2026  
**Type:** Official statistical data  
**URL:** [https://microdata.nigerianstat.gov.ng/index.php/catalog/154]

### Why this source was selected

The NBS CPI provides official data needed to verify claims about inflation and consumer-price changes in Nigeria.

### Evidence used in this project

- Headline inflation
- Food inflation
- Core inflation
- Year-on-year inflation
- Month-on-month inflation

### Evaluation use

This source is primarily used to test:

- factual accuracy
- numerical interpretation
- understanding of inflation measures
- distinction between changes in prices and changes in the inflation rate



## Source 5: National Bureau of Statistics

**Title:** Premium Motor Spirit (Petrol) Price Watch — February 2026  
**Organization:** National Bureau of Statistics (NBS)  
**Publication date:** February 2026  
**Type:** Official statistical data  
**URL:** [https://microdata.nigerianstat.gov.ng/index.php/catalog/157/related-materials]

### Why this source was selected

The PMS Price Watch provides official data on petrol retail prices across Nigeria and allows fuel-price claims in other sources to be checked against primary statistical evidence.

### Evidence used in this project

- Average petrol retail price in February 2026
- Comparison with February 2025
- Comparison with January 2026
- State and zonal price information where relevant

### Evaluation use

This source is primarily used to test:

- factual accuracy
- numerical reasoning
- source verification
- interpretation of changes in petrol prices
