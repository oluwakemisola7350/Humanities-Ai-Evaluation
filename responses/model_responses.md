QUESTION:

Reuters reports on the removal of Nigeria's fuel subsidy and subsequent increases in petrol prices, transportation costs, and household expenses. Using the Reuters report and the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) data, evaluate whether the available evidence is sufficient to conclude that the removal of the fuel subsidy directly caused Nigeria's subsequent inflation.

In your answer:

1. distinguish between temporal association, a plausible contributing mechanism, and evidence sufficient to establish causation;
2. identify at least two alternative factors that could have contributed to inflation; and
3. support your reasoning with evidence from the cited sources.]
  

**Model response:**

[Based strictly on the provided sources, the available evidence is not sufficient to establish that the removal of Nigeria's fuel subsidy directly caused the subsequent inflation.

1. Distinction Between Temporal Association, Plausible Mechanism, and Causation

* Temporal Association: The sources indicate that the subsidy was removed and that increases in petrol prices, transportation costs, household expenses, and overall consumer prices (CPI) occurred subsequently. However, observing event B (inflation) after event A (subsidy removal) only establishes a sequence in time, not a direct cause-and-effect relationship.

* Plausible Contributing Mechanism: The Reuters report describes a logical pathway through which subsidy removal could drive up costs: abolishing the subsidy leads to higher petrol prices, which directly increases transportation costs, thereby raising household expenses and overall living costs. This demonstrates a plausible transmission mechanism, but a plausible mechanism alone does not prove that it was the sole or primary driver of overall inflation.

* Sufficient Evidence for Causation: Establishing direct causation requires proving that the subsidy removal directly generated the broader inflation while isolating or controlling for all other macroeconomic factors. The cited sources do not provide counterfactual analysis, statistical isolation, or econometric models required to isolate the subsidy removal from other concurrent economic variables. Therefore, claiming direct causation exceeds what the provided evidence establishes.

2. Alternative Contributing Factors
Based on macroeconomic context and the data provided by the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS) and Reuters, multiple alternative or compounding factors could have contributed to Nigeria's inflation during this period:

* Currency Devaluation / Exchange Rate Fluctuations: Changes in foreign exchange policy and currency depreciation significantly increase the import costs of goods, raw materials, and refined petroleum products, driving broad-based price increases across the consumer basket tracked in the Consumer Price Index (CPI).

* Monetary Policy and Money Supply Growth: Broad money supply expansion and central bank financing can drive demand-pull inflation across all household expenses, independent of specific commodity price shocks like fuel.
(Note: While the provided sources track price indices and report on fuel subsidy protests, they do not isolate these broader macroeconomic variables, meaning we cannot rule them out as primary or joint drivers of the observed inflation.)

3. Summary of Reasoning Supported by the Sources

* Supported by Sources: The Reuters report confirms the policy change (subsidy removal) and documents the resulting surge in petrol prices, transport fares, and household costs. The NBS CPI and Petrol Price Watch datasets record the numerical increases in consumer prices and fuel costs across the country over time.

* Inference vs. Direct Support: Inferring that fuel prices played a role in rising household expenses is a logical deduction supported by the transmission mechanism described in the Reuters report. However, concluding that the subsidy removal caused the total macroeconomic inflation requires an ungrounded leap from temporal correlation to definitive causation—a claim explicitly unsupported by the provided evidence.
